Course at a Glance

Human-AI Interaction · Designing Intelligent Systems that Work for Humans

This course introduces the core principles of human-centered design for conversational AI systems — chatbots, virtual assistants, and interactive voice interfaces. You’ll explore how NLP, dialogue management, and UX principles converge to create intuitive, accessible, and inclusive AI interactions.

“Designing AI that works for humans — in all their wonderful diversity.”

Course Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, you will be able to…

01 — Critique & Synthesize Research Review scientific literature in human-AI interaction to synthesize trends and inform evidence-based design decisions.

02 — Design Conversational Systems Apply human-centered design alongside NLP and dialogue management to prototype functional, context-aware conversational interfaces.

03 — Evaluate Usability & Ethics Conduct rigorous usability testing to assess user trust, efficiency, and error recovery, while analyzing ethical risks.

04 — Collaborate & Communicate Work in multidisciplinary teams to manage interactive AI projects, communicating outcomes professionally.

Project 1: VR Learning Environment

Co-designing and authoring a 9-screen VR intervention for older adults, validated on the Meta Quest 3.

Design Considerations for Older Adults (70+)

  • Large UI targets — generous touch/pointer areas
  • High-contrast visual cues that remain legible
  • Gentle pacing — no time pressure
  • Audio support — instructions read aloud
  • Low-stress error recovery — no penalties
  • Minimal cognitive load — one action per screen

Example: Medication Management

  1. Why Medication Schedules Matter
  2. Inspect the Pill Bottle
  3. Food vs. Empty Stomach
  4. Morning Routine Steps
  5. Emergency Kit Checklist
  6. Key Terms & Definitions
  7. Storage Best Practices
  8. Handling Missed Doses
  9. Reminder Alarm Logic

Course Policies

PROFESSIONALISM & INTEGRITY

Attendance, late work, generative AI use, and academic honesty — know these before Week 1.

Attendance & Late Submission

Attendance & Participation

  • Attend all classes, labs, and workshops — come prepared to contribute meaningfully.
  • Attendance is tracked randomly through in-class participation activities.
  • Participation activities cannot be made up if missed.
  • Unable to attend? Notify the instructor as soon as possible.

Late Submission: Not Allowed Without Approval

  • Email the instructor before the deadline explaining the situation.
  • Receive a written reply confirming any extension.
  • No lateness beyond 24 hours is tolerated without explicit approval.
  • Maximum of 2 late submissions allowed across the entire course.

Policy on AI & Generative AI Use

This course heavily integrates AI — use it responsibly, and always disclose it.

AI is encouraged for…

  • Explaining complex concepts
  • Providing coding examples
  • Offering debugging suggestions
  • Recommending best practices
  • Exploring new ideas and approaches

Responsible use requires: never using AI to solve entire assignments, always acknowledging AI usage, understanding everything you submit.

The perils of over-reliance

  • Hinders your learning — hands-on problem-solving is irreplaceable
  • Impedes your growth — prevents critical thinking development
  • Limits your creativity — over-dependence stifles innovation
  • Compromises your career — employers value independent thinkers

Academic Honesty for CS Students

Reasonable

  • Talking with classmates about assignments
  • Discussing strategies verbally (no code shared)
  • Whiteboarding a solution together
  • Searching the web for general concepts
  • Small cited code snippets
  • Working with a tutor on your own code

Careful

  • Showing code to another student for debugging only, in private
  • Reviewing others’ code after submission to enhance learning

Not Reasonable

  • Submitting another’s work as your own
  • Copying from a quiz or exam
  • Viewing or copying another’s solution
  • Copying solutions from previous years
  • Recording or sharing exam content
  • Maintaining public repositories of solutions

Our Value Statement

This course is designed for students from all disciplinary backgrounds — not just Computer Science. We believe diverse perspectives are essential for designing AI systems that work for everyone.

Students of different abilities, identities, and disciplinary backgrounds bring unique value to human-centered AI design. Your perspective on how AI systems affect your community is not just welcome — it is essential to the course’s intellectual mission.

“The goal is not to build AI that thinks like humans, but AI that works for humans — in all their wonderful diversity.”
