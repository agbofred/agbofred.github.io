How to Read Academic Papers

Reading HCI research papers is a skill. For each required paper, aim to:

  1. Skim first — abstract, intro, conclusions, figures (5 min)
  2. Identify the main claim — what do the authors argue, and what evidence supports it?
  3. Note what surprised you — bring one observation to class
  4. Assess transferability — how does this apply to your project or a system you know?

For long readings, focus on the sections indicated in the schedule. Full papers are provided for context.

Title Authors Year Type Access

Rec Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction Amershi et al. 2019 CHI Paper ACM DL
Rec Human-AI Interaction & Generative AI — 2024 Survey ACM DL

Reading note for Norman: Focus on Norman’s concept of “affordances” and “signifiers.” Ask yourself: do AI systems have signifiers? How does a chatbot communicate what it can do?

Reading note for DiSalvo: The six genres are (1) human factors, (2) cognitive engineering, (3) human-computer interaction, (4) computer-supported cooperative work, (5) participatory design, and (6) critical computing. Where do conversational AI systems fit? Multiple genres?

Reading note on Persuasion: Fogg distinguishes between kairos (persuasion through opportune timing), reduction (simplifying behavior), and tunneling (guiding users through a sequence). Consider: which of these does your AI system prototype use?

Reading note on Dijkstra: Written in 1978, Dijkstra’s essay argues that using natural language for programming is a terrible idea. In 2026 — with LLMs enabling natural language programming — revisit his arguments. Which ones hold? Which don’t? This is an excellent lens for thinking about the affordances and limitations of conversational AI.

Rec Chatbot Usability Evaluation: The BUS Scale Borsci et al. 2022 Paper Canvas PDF

Reading note on Heuristics: Nielsen’s 10 heuristics were developed for GUI systems. For AI/conversational systems, additional heuristics have been proposed: “Does the system communicate uncertainty?”, “Can users understand why the AI made a decision?”, “Does the system fail gracefully?” Look for these in Jain et al. and think about which apply to your project.

Key Conferences & Journals

Staying current with the research literature is part of being an informed HCI practitioner. These are the key venues publishing Human-AI Interaction research:

Venue Type Scope Notes
CHI (ACM CHI Conference) Conference Flagship HCI venue Annual, competitive; many assigned papers are from here
CSCW Conference Collaborative & social computing Especially relevant for team AI, platforms, and social systems
IUI Conference Intelligent User Interfaces Specifically focused on the intersection of AI and UI design
UIST Conference Novel UI systems and technology Strong prototyping and systems focus
FAccT Conference Fairness, accountability, transparency Essential for AI ethics readings
TOCHI Journal ACM Transactions on CHI Premier journal for HCI research
IJHCI Journal International Journal of HCI Broad coverage of HCI theory and practice

How to Access Papers

  1. Canvas: All required PDFs are uploaded to the course Canvas site under the “Readings” module
  2. Willamette Library: library.willamette.edu — use your WU credentials to access ACM Digital Library and IEEE Xplore
  3. Google Scholar: Many papers have free preprints; search the title and look for PDF links
  4. ArXiv: arxiv.org — free preprints for many recent AI/HCI papers
  5. Semantic Scholar: semanticscholar.org — free access to many papers with citation graphs
Tip

Can’t access a paper? Post in the #readings-help Discord channel. The instructor or a classmate can usually help within 24 hours.
