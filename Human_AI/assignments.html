HW0 — Personal Introduction & Expectations

Due: Thursday, Aug 28 · Canvas Discussion Board · Participation

Objective

Instructions

HW1 — Critical Literature Synthesis & Interface Critique

Due: Friday, Sep 5 (11:59 PM) · 3%

Description

Write a 300-word position paper analyzing an AI tool through the lens of the Week 1–2 readings.

Tasks

  1. Select an intelligent everyday application (e.g., ChatGPT, email smart reply, or a recommendation engine).
  2. Critique the application using the tension between Direct Manipulation vs. Interface Agents (Shneiderman & Maes, 1997) and Power to the People (Amershi et al., 2014).
  3. Evaluate how effectively the tool supports human agency versus over-automating decisions.
  4. Propose two concrete UX redesigns to resolve the identified issues.

Submission

Submit a PDF to Canvas. Include screenshots to support your critique.

Grading Rubric

Criterion Points
Clear identification of the AI system and its role 10
Accurate application of Direct Manipulation vs. Agents debate 20
Accurate application of “Power to the People” framework 20
Quality of two redesign proposals (specific and justified) 30
Writing clarity and organization 10
Adherence to 300-word scope 10
Total 100 points

HW2 — Applied Needfinding & Low-Fidelity Prototype

Due: Thursday, Sep 18 (11:59 PM) · 3%

Description

Produce a 2–3 page project discovery report and an initial paper prototype for an individual design concept.

Tasks

  1. Observation & Persona — Conduct a brief participant observation session (15–20 min) of a user interacting with an existing intelligent application. Construct 1–2 user personas based on observed pain points, referencing Klemmer’s observation video (Week 3) and Visme’s persona-creation method.

  2. Problem Critique — Identify user-reported issues (loss of control, unpredictability, or lack of transparency) inspired by When People and Algorithms Meet (Eslami et al., IUI 2019).

  3. Ideation & Prototyping — Use a brainstorming technique (Chainstorm or Tweetstorm from Week 4 readings) to design an alternative interface. Create a low-fidelity paper prototype demonstrating a mixed-initiative approach balancing agent automation with user control.

Deliverables

  • 2–3 page written report (personas, problem statement, design rationale)
  • Photographs or scans of paper prototype (minimum 3 distinct screens/states)

Grading Rubric

Criterion Points
Observation quality and persona grounding in evidence 20
Problem critique linked to Eslami et al. (IUI 2019) 20
Prototype coverage and annotation 25
Design rationale connecting research to choices 25
Writing quality 10
Total 100 points

GP1 — Co-Designing & Authoring VR Interventions for Older Adults

Due: Friday, Oct 2 · 15%
Submission: Group Website Post + Demo Video Link + Authoring System Export

Objective

Engage directly with older adults (ages 70+) to co-design an interactive VR intervention supporting cognitive health, memory, or daily routine management. Teams move from real-world needfinding through paper prototyping to authoring a functional 9-screen VR experience on the course web-authoring tool, validated on the Meta Quest 3.

Phase 1 — Needfinding & Unplugged Ideation

  • Engage target users — Interview or observe at least one older adult (70+) to understand daily routines, cognitive challenges (pill management, short-term recall, spatial awareness), or mobility goals.
  • Paper prototyping — Design an “unplugged” 9-step activity using paper cards, state diagrams, or storyboards before using the authoring tool. Map the full sequence, state transitions, and user prompts.

Phase 2 — Digital Authoring & Orchestration

  • Platform access — http://147.182.173.208:5173/
  • Author your VR lesson — Translate your paper design into a 9-screen VR experience for the Meta Quest 3. Incorporate: large UI targets, high-contrast visuals, gentle pacing, low-stress error recovery.

Phase 3 — VR Validation & First-Person Testing

  • In-headset playback — Run through your 9-screen lesson wearing the Meta Quest 3.
  • Record screen capture — Use Quest 3’s built-in recorder to capture a complete narrated playthrough.

Reference 9-Screen Scenario: Medication Management

Screen Type Activity Title
1 Explanation Why Medication Schedules Matter
2 Exploration Inspect the Pill Bottle
3 Organization – Sorting Food vs. Empty Stomach
4 Organization – Ordering Morning Routine Steps
5 Organization – Selection Emergency Kit Checklist
6 Matching Key Terms & Definitions
7 Sentence Completion Storage Best Practices
8 Multiple Choice Handling Missed Doses
9 Vertical Block Dragging Reminder Alarm Logic

Submission Deliverables (Team Website + Canvas)

  1. Needfinding & Unplugged Artifacts — Photos of paper prototypes, state diagrams, user notes
  2. 9-Screen System Mapping Table — Activity flow, inputs/outputs, UX considerations per screen
  3. VR Demonstration Video — All 9 screens in Meta Quest 3 with narration (YouTube/Vimeo/MP4)
  4. Authoring System File/Link — JSON export or link from the web-authoring platform

Grading

Criterion Points
Needfinding evidence and older adult engagement 20
Paper prototype completeness and state mapping 20
9-screen VR experience quality and UX considerations 30
Demonstration video (all screens, narrated, clear) 20
Website/report organization 10
Total 100 points

Final Reflection Essay

Due: Saturday, Dec 5 · 10%

Description

Write an individual reflection essay (800–1,200 words). This is a reflective synthesis — not a summary of the course.

Address all four prompts:

  1. Most important insight — What is the most important thing you learned about designing AI systems for human use? Give a concrete example from your project or the course.
  2. Changed thinking — How did your perspective on AI and society shift over the semester? What challenged your assumptions?
  3. Ethical dimension — Choose one ethical issue in AI design (fairness, transparency, privacy, manipulation, access) and describe how you would address it as a designer.
  4. Looking forward — What aspect of Human-AI Interaction do you most want to explore further? What question do you wish this course had answered?

Submission

Submit individually via Canvas. Do not share with teammates before submitting.

Grading

Criterion Points
Depth and specificity of reflection 30
Connection to course concepts and readings 25
Evidence of changed or nuanced thinking 20
Writing quality (clarity, voice, flow) 15
Forward-looking insight 10
Total 100 points
Tip

Strong essays make specific references to readings, in-class activities, or project experiences — not just generic statements about AI. Show your thinking process, not just your conclusions.
